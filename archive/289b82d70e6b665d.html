Positive Declaration, Availability of Draft Scope and Public Scoping Session

Kings County - The New York City Department of Transportation (NYC DOT), as lead agency, has determined that the proposed Brooklyn-Queens Expressway/Interstate 278 (“BQE”) from Atlantic Avenue to Nassau Street Project (“BQE Central” or the “Project”) may have a significant adverse impact on the environment and a Draft Environmental Impact Statement must be prepared. 

The Draft Scope is available from the contact listed below and online at: www.nyc.gov/bqe. An in-person public scoping session will be held on November 4, 2026 from 6:30 PM to 8:30 PM at Saint Francis College, 179 Livingston Street, Brooklyn, New York. A virtual public scoping session will be held on November 5, 2026 from 6:30 PM to 8:30 PM via Zoom. Information on how to join and comment is available at www.nyc.gov/bqe. Written comments on the Draft Scope will be accepted through November 16, 2026.

NYC DOT is proposing the rehabilitation of the BQE from Atlantic Avenue to Nassau Street, in the Borough of Brooklyn. The purpose of the Project is to address identified structural deficiencies of BQE Central and maintain the regional vehicular connectivity and mobility that the highway provides. The Project involves an approximately 1.5-mile section of the BQE from just north of Atlantic Avenue to Nassau Street. The project limits include the BQE Central roadway, shoulders and offsets, support structures, Furman Street, the Brooklyn Heights Promenade, local street overpasses and underpasses between Atlantic Avenue and Nassau Street, and adjacent New York City-owned property, including parking lots, maintenance and storage areas, and open space. Within the project limits, the BQE parallels the eastern and southern shore of the East River through a densely developed urban area, with a wide range of land uses in proximity to the project limits.

BQE Central includes the unique 0.4-mile-long triple cantilever section. The structure takes advantage of an extreme change in elevation between the Brooklyn waterfront and Brooklyn Heights. It is composed of two levels of highway and the Brooklyn Heights Promenade on a structure elevated above Furman Street. The structure hangs from a retaining wall that extends from ground level to the top of the bluff. The cantilever section begins at Joralemon Street just north of Atlantic Avenue and ends at Washington Street.

The Project would involve a major rehabilitation of all 21 structures within the project limits to address structural deficiencies and increase their service life. The New York State Historic Preservation Office (SHPO) has determined the portion of the BQE between Brooklyn Heights and the Kosciuszko Bridge to be eligible for listing on the State and National Registers of Historic Places (S/NR-eligible). In addition, the Brooklyn Heights Promenade is within the Brooklyn Heights Historic District, which the New York City Landmarks Preservation Commission (LPC) has designated a New York City Landmark district; the district is also S/NR-listed. The Project would generally retain the historic integrity of the highway within the project limits, including the triple cantilever structure. 

NYC DOT would replace the superstructure of the Columbia Heights overpass and relocate and reconstruct its north abutment. NYC DOT would rehabilitate all the other bridges and structures within the project limits to extend their service life. At the triple cantilever section, the Project would maintain the existing mainline and ramp alignments, lane widths, shoulder widths, horizontal and vertical clearances, and other roadway geometric features. With the Project, BQE Central would continue to operate as it does today, with two lanes in each direction except for a short section of the highway between the Manhattan Bridge and just south of Sands Street which operates with three full lanes in the Staten Island-bound direction only. East of Adams Street, NYC DOT would explore addressing non-standard features where practicable. The Project would correct current drainage issues and would provide a new bridge railing that meets current design standards. 

The project limits include a portion of the property at 25 Joralemon Street. NYC DOT would acquire the portion of the property that is vacant and encased within the existing BQE structure. NYC DOT would not acquire the occupied portion of this structure that is immediately east of the BQE. The project limits also include small sections of mapped parkland adjacent to the roadway, which NYC DOT would incorporate through alienation legislation by the New York State legislature, including a home rule action by the New York City Council. The Project would require City Map amendments to account for these alterations to land use.

To fully rehabilitate the structure, NYC DOT would construct two temporary bypass structures alongside the current highway that would carry traffic during construction. One bypass structure would parallel the triple cantilever section from just north of Atlantic Avenue to Columbia Heights; this structure would be located within the Furman Street right-of-way below the promenade elevation. A second bypass structure would carry traffic from Columbia Heights, potentially above the Brooklyn Bridge, and through Dumbo, tying back into the highway structure just west of the Manhattan Bridge. Upon the completion of construction, NYC DOT would demolish both temporary structures.

NYC DOT anticipates that construction would commence in 2029 and would be completed before 2040. NYC DOT continues to develop the construction means and methods for the Project. As part of these efforts, NYC DOT will explore innovations aimed at shortening the duration of construction.
